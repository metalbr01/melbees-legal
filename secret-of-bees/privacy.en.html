Privacy Policy — Melbees: The Secret of Bees

Effective date: 2026-07-27 · Last updated: 2026-10-10

1. Who we are

Melbees (“the app”, “we”) is provided by Melbees Games, Brazil, which is the controller of the data described in this policy. For any privacy question or request, write to contact@melbees.games.

2. Scope

This policy explains what information Melbees handles, how, and what your choices are. It applies to the Melbees game distributed on Google Play (Android) and on the Microsoft Store (Windows), in every country where it is available. The Windows version handles less data than the Android one, and section 2.1 gathers every difference; where it says nothing, the rest of this policy applies. If the game is ever distributed through another store, this policy will be updated first.

Depending on where you live, besides Brazil's LGPD (Law 13.709/2018) the GDPR of the European Union and the United Kingdom, the United States' COPPA and other local laws may apply to you. Section 15 describes the rights each of them gives you and how to use them.

2.1 The Windows version (Microsoft Store)

The Windows version is the same game, and it handles less data. What changes in it:

  • There are no ads at all. That is why the game never asks your age: the question in section 5 exists only because of the ad. Nothing in section 6 happens on Windows.
  • There is no Firebase. The Windows version has neither Google Analytics for Firebase nor Firebase Crashlytics: no game event and no crash report is sent (section 10 applies to Android only).
  • Microsoft does the charging, through the Microsoft Store purchase system, on your Microsoft account. The items are those of section 7 plus a 1-Petal pack. We never see or receive your payment details. From Petal purchases, nothing reaches us. From the Premium subscription, something reaches us only if you signed in with the account (section 8), and it is this:
    • The Microsoft Store ID key. The game asks our server for a single-use authorization and, with it, asks the Microsoft Store for a key: a code Microsoft issues for your Microsoft account that lets our server look up the Melbees subscriptions on that account. Microsoft receives with it a code we derive from your Melbees account that does not reveal who you are; it makes the key good for that account only. The computer where you subscribed sends a new key each time it checks the subscription with the account signed in.
    • What we keep from the subscription, together with the key and the link to your account: the subscription's identifier at Microsoft, its state (active, with a payment pending, or ended) and the date it ends. On each check we also read whether it renews by itself and whether it is in the free trial, and we pass that on to the game without keeping it. The country of purchase and the rest of what Microsoft answers are never kept. Each link also writes a line to the purchase audit log (section 14).
    • For how long. The key lasts 30 days, and the server renews it before it ends while it checks the subscription; an expired key is deleted. The key and the link are deleted when you delete the account (section 15).
    We use this to unlock Premium on your devices and to confirm the subscription with Microsoft, against fraud. Cancellations, refunds and the right of withdrawal are requested from Microsoft, on your Microsoft account, because Microsoft is the seller.
  • The account and the online leaderboard work as on Android (sections 8 and 9), with the same Google account. What changes is the sign-in: Sign in with Google opens your browser, where Google asks for the account; the browser hands the game a single-use code, which the game exchanges for the session at Supabase, our authentication processor (section 13). The session stays in your Windows user, in the computer's local folder (never the folder that travels between computers), and encrypted twice by Windows: one layer only your Windows user opens and one layer only this computer opens. A copy taken to another computer or another user does not open, and the game deletes it. The session is erased when you tap Sign out, Delete my data or Delete my account. The Google session inside the browser belongs to the browser: the game neither reads nor erases it.
  • Feedback works as on Android (section 11); in the technical block, the platform shows as Windows.
  • On the computer, your progress and settings stay in your Windows user and are never sent to us. Settings → Delete my data deletes all of it, as on Android.
  • The parental controls are Microsoft's: Microsoft Family Safety can limit screen time and require approval before a purchase on the Microsoft Store (section 12).

3. The summary, in five lines

  • Playing is offline. All of your progress stays on the device and is never sent to us.
  • We do not ask your age in order to let you play, and there is no date-of-birth screen. We ask your age once, before the first ad, and we keep only the band — never the number (section 5). On Windows there is no ad, and your age is never asked (section 2.1).
  • An account is optional and is only asked for when you choose to enter the online leaderboard or to restore your subscription on another device, the other platform included (section 8). On Windows the account is the same, and signing in happens in the browser (section 2.1).
  • We do not sell your data, we do not trade it with data brokers, and we do not build advertising profiles out of what you play.
  • You can delete everything: the device data from inside the game, and the account from the game or from a web page, without needing the game installed (section 15).

4. What stays only on your device

Melbees keeps the following on your device only, so the game can work; none of it is sent to us or to any server:

  • Game progress: the secrets you uncovered (your Apiary), best times, phases, feats, items and unlocks.
  • Settings: language, sound, tap mode, theme and anything else you choose.
  • The age band you answered before the ad — only under 13, 13 to 17 or 18 or over (section 5).
  • Your answer to the ad-consent request, when it is shown (section 6). It is Google's own SDK that stores it.
  • Your play time on this device, which the “For parents and guardians” page shows (section 12). That counter never leaves the device.
  • Which of the game team's messages you have read in Hug's Mail (section 12). The game never tells us what was read.

Deleting the game, or using Settings → Delete my data, removes all of it.

If you signed in with Google, the device also keeps your account session, as section 8 describes.

5. Children, teenagers and the age question

Melbees is a general-audience, family-friendly game, and we know that children and teenagers play it. That is why the version of the game everyone gets is already the version a minor is allowed to have: there is no chat, no messaging between players, no nickname you type on the leaderboard, no paid random reward, and no advertising profile built out of what you play. The game team's messages (section 12) are one-way: nobody answers them and no player writes one.

There is no age screen at the door and no parental gate. A date-of-birth form is trivially bypassed by a child, and a weak control gives parents a safety that does not exist. The strong control is the parents', at the platform: Google Family Link and Google Play's purchase approval. Section 12 says where they are. On Windows, the control is Microsoft Family Safety (section 2.1).

The one thing that needs an age is the ad, because the advertising rule is different for each band. So, the first time an ad is about to be shown, the game asks your age once:

  • It is a free field, with nothing preselected, and a Skip button of the same weight. We do not say which answer changes what, so that nobody is nudged into lying.
  • We keep only the band — under 13, 13 to 17 or 18 or over. The number you typed is discarded and is never stored or transmitted.
  • Skipping blocks nothing. You keep playing and you still get the ad's Petal; the band simply stays unknown, and an unknown band gets the most restricted treatment, the same one a child gets.
  • The band is used for exactly two things, both on your device: choosing the treatment of the ad request (section 6), and deciding whether analytics may keep an installation identifier, which happens only at 18 or over (section 10). Crash reports do not depend on it: they are sent for everyone. It does not enter any statistic, it does not go to any server of ours or of Google's, it does not change price, offer or content, and it is not used to profile anyone.
  • We do not ask the platform. Google offers an age signal derived from the account (Play Age Signals) and we do not query it: its terms forbid using that information for advertising, which is the only thing we would need an age for.

United States. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13 for the purposes COPPA restricts. European Union and United Kingdom. Some countries set the age at which a teenager can consent alone anywhere between 13 and 16; where that limit is higher than ours, ad personalisation depends on the valid consent described in section 6.

If you are a parent or guardian and believe a child has sent us personal information — through the feedback screen, for instance — write to contact@melbees.games and we will delete it.

6. Ads

The game shows rewarded ads, and only those. There is no banner, no interstitial, and no ad ever appears by itself: one is shown only after you tap to earn an extra Petal when you lose your last one. If you never tap, you will never see an ad. The Windows version has no ads at all (section 2.1): this section applies to Android only.

Ads are served by Google AdMob, which acts as an independent controller of the data it receives. The treatment of each request depends on the band from section 5:

  • Under 13, or an unknown band (you skipped, or have not answered yet): the request is marked child-directed, the ad is capped at the lowest content rating, personalisation is off, and the device's advertising identifier (AAID) is not sent.
  • 13 to 17: a non-personalised ad.
  • 18 or over: the ad may be personalised — and only is if you consent. The consent request is Google's own dialog (User Messaging Platform), shown according to the rules of your country; in the European Union and the United Kingdom nothing is personalised without your consent, and you can change it later through the same dialog.

For a personalised ad, AdMob receives your device's advertising identifier — an identifier you can reset or delete yourself in the Android settings, under Google → Ads. We do not receive it, we do not store it, and we do not use it anywhere else in the game, analytics included (section 10).

7. In-app purchases

The game offers three items, and none of them is required to play: two Petal packs (one-off purchases) and the monthly Premium subscription. There is no loot box: you always know exactly what you are buying, and nothing bought turns into a draw. On Windows the Microsoft Store does the charging, and there is also a 1-Petal pack (section 2.1); the list below describes Google Play.

  • Google Play does the charging, through its own billing system. We never see or receive your payment details — card, billing address or any part of them.
  • What reaches us is the purchase receipt Google issues: a purchase token, the product identifier and, for the subscription, its state (active, cancelled, in a trial, refunded). We use that to unlock what you bought, to acknowledge the purchase to Google, and to stop one receipt being used on several accounts.
  • Buying does not require an account. If you have signed in (section 8), the subscription is bound to your account so that you can restore it on another device, from the same store or the other one: with the same account, a Google Play subscription also counts on Windows, and a Microsoft Store one also counts on Android, unless that use is turned off (Terms, section 5).
  • Cancellation and refunds belong to Google Play, under the subscriptions of your Google account. Deleting your Melbees account does not cancel the subscription.
  • Right of withdrawal (European Union and United Kingdom). You have 14 days to withdraw from a digital purchase, unless you expressly agreed to immediate performance and acknowledged losing that right — which Google Play's own purchase screen presents. The request goes to Google Play, which is the seller.

8. The account, and when it is asked for

The account is optional, and you need none to play or to buy. The game never asks you to sign in on launch. It offers the sign-in for two things only, and signing in is always an action of yours: the online leaderboard and restoring a subscription on another device, the other platform included. A subscriber who has not signed in also sees that offer as a quiet line before a game, saying it will not reach the online leaderboard without the account, and as a button on the screen that celebrates the subscription. Neither stops you from playing.

On Windows the account is the same, and signing in opens the browser; section 2.1 describes that sign-in and where Windows keeps the session.

The account is Google only: no e-mail-and-password sign-up, no anonymous login. The sign-in is run by Supabase, our authentication processor (section 13). When you sign in, Google confirms to Supabase that it is you, and Supabase keeps, on the sign-in server, with your account:

  • an opaque account identifier, a meaningless string. It is the only thing the game and the leaderboard use to recognise you;
  • the e-mail address of your Google account, and the name and the profile picture link Google sends with it (the picture stays at Google; Supabase keeps only the link);
  • the date the account was created and the date of the last sign-in;
  • for the account's security, the IP address and the kind of device and browser of each sign-in and of each session renewal, in an access record.

The game reads none of this except the identifier. Your name, e-mail and picture never appear on the leaderboard or to another player, never go into analytics or ads, and are not sold or shared. We look at the e-mail only to answer a request of yours: to find your account when you ask by e-mail to delete it (Delete your account) or when you ask for a courtesy Premium. All of it is deleted when you delete the account (section 15). Supabase also keeps a copy of the access record in its own technical logs, which it deletes by itself within a few days.

Courtesy Premium. If you ask us for a courtesy Premium and send us the e-mail of your Google account, we use it only to find your account, in our administration panel, when we grant or renew the courtesy. The courtesy does not keep the e-mail: we keep only its start, end and reason, tied to the account's opaque identifier, and we delete them when you delete the account. The message in which you sent us the e-mail stays in our mailbox, like any message you send us.

So that you do not have to sign in again every time you open the game, the session is kept on this device: only the credential that renews it, never your name or your e-mail. On Android, it is encrypted, with a key Android creates and that never leaves the device, and it sits in a place Android keeps out of backups and out of transfers to another device, so your session never shows up on a device you did not sign in on. It is erased when you tap Sign out, Delete my data or Delete my account, or when you uninstall the game. On Windows, section 2.1 says where it is kept. The session is used only when something needs it, such as the online leaderboard. Opening the game does not connect to the account, with one exception: when the device holds the answer about the account's Premium (below) and its date has passed, the game renews the session by itself, with no screen, to ask again.

Premium follows the account. With the account signed in, and when this device's store has no subscription, the game asks our server whether the account has Premium, bought on Google Play or on the Microsoft Store. The server answers which store it is from and until when it lasts. The device keeps that answer for that account, and it counts until that date, so you can play as Premium even offline. A newer answer from the server replaces it, and it is erased when you tap Sign out, Delete my data or Delete my account, or when another account signs in on this device.

A message from the team addressed to your account (section 12) is kept on our server, tied only to the account's opaque identifier, and is deleted with the account. The game asks for it only with your session open; when you sign out, it stops showing.

9. The online leaderboard

If — and only if — you enter the online leaderboard, this leaves your device:

  • Your run: the total time, the category, the language, the difficulty, the date and the list of moves (what you revealed, when, and which cards you used). We re-run it on the server to confirm the time is real — that is how the leaderboard defends itself against cheating. It contains nothing about you, only about the run.
  • A nickname built from the game's own pieces, of the “Thunder Isa #4821” kind: one of the game's characters, a surname from a closed list the game writes in each language, and a number the server draws for your account, which never changes and is never given to anyone else. You pick the character and the surname from those lists and never type a letter: there is no free-text field, so no nickname can carry personal data and no player can write to another. The first nickname is drawn for you when you first sign in.
  • A portrait you choose among the game's characters and their skins. It is the game's own art, never a photo, and carries nothing about you.

What the public sees is only the best marks of each board: rank, nickname, number, portrait and time. No identification of who played appears there, neither to other players nor to anyone looking from outside.

The raw list of moves is deleted 60 days after submission; the time and the rank stay for as long as your account exists. Our game server does not store your IP address in any table: it uses it in memory, at the instant of the call, to rate-limit requests. Google Cloud, which hosts the server as our processor, records each request made to it (the IP address, the kind of device, the time and the address called) in a technical log that it deletes by itself after 30 days; we look at it only to investigate a failure or an attack. The sign-in keeps the IP separately, in the account's access record (section 8).

10. Analytics and crash reports

This section applies to Android only: the Windows version does not use Firebase (section 2.1).

We use Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics to know whether the game works and where it breaks. They are configured as internal operations, and that configuration is the reason we can use them with every player:

  • Advertising-identifier collection is off, explicitly, for everyone.
  • Ad-personalisation signals are off and Google Signals is off. None of it is linked to AdMob or to advertising audiences.
  • Your age band never becomes a statistic: it is not sent, it is not a dimension and it appears in no event.
  • No text you write enters an event — not a name, not an e-mail, not your feedback message.

What is recorded are game events, such as a run started or finished, the Premium screen being seen, a purchase completed, a Petal obtained by ad, by purchase or for free, and a subscription starting or lapsing. The events are the same for every player. Alongside them go those services' standard technical data: the device model, the system version, the country and the language.

What changes with age, and only this. Before the first ad, the game asks your age (section 6) and keeps only the band.

  • If you did not answer, skipped, or answered under 18: the events go without any identifier. Firebase Analytics runs in consent mode with every storage denied, so it sends no device or installation identifier with them.
  • If you answered 18 or over: Firebase Analytics keeps a per-installation identifier, which it generates itself and which disappears when you uninstall the game.

Crash reports are the same for everyone. Whatever your age, and whether or not you answered, when the game fails Crashlytics sends a crash report: the error's stack trace, the state of the game at that moment and the device's technical data above. The only identifier it carries is an installation identifier, made of a random number Crashlytics generates itself and Firebase's installation number; it identifies this installation of the game, not you. It carries no name, no e-mail, no text you wrote and not your age band.

Your age band decides what analytics keeps on your device and is not sent. Settings → Delete my data also reaches Firebase: it stops sending the game's errors to Crashlytics at once, erases Firebase's installation number (on the device and on Firebase's servers), denies every storage again and erases from the device the analytics data, before erasing the rest. Crashlytics stays off until you close the game. The next time the game opens, it first erases any crash report still waiting on the device, unsent; then Crashlytics works again as for everyone, with a new installation identifier. If a report had to be erased, Crashlytics stays off in that opening, and each later opening checks again; it works again from the first one that finds nothing waiting. Erasing the installation number needs an internet connection: without one, the game tells you the deletion was not complete, and you can try again. After it, the game treats you as someone who has not answered.

What this does not erase. What had already been sent stays with Google, tied to identifiers that no longer exist on your device, and is erased by the retention period: 2 months for analytics data and 90 days for crash reports.

11. Feedback and support

The game has a Send feedback screen (Settings ▸ Account) and a per-word report on the results screen. They exist so you can tell us about a problem, a wrong word, an idea or a compliment. There is no automatic sending: nothing leaves the device until you tap “Send”.

What is sent, and nothing beyond it: the category you chose (one of four fixed labels), your message as free text, and a technical block the app assembles and shows you on screen before you send — the app version and build type, the platform and the operating-system version string cut at 40 characters (on Android this is usually a kernel line, not the device model), the interface language, the tap mode, the screen you were on, and a list of up to 20 recent actions as internal game codes. Errors appear as a label from a closed list, never the error message and never the stack. A per-word report also carries the category, subcategory, secret count, phase and the word's internal identifier.

Important: the message field is free text and reaches us exactly as you wrote it. Do not write your name, e-mail, phone number, address or any other personal information there — yours or a child's. We do not need it to fix the game, and the app says so under the field.

Where it goes. The report is sent over HTTPS to a Google Apps Script service operated by Melbees Games, which appends it as a row in a Google Sheets spreadsheet of ours, with access restricted to us. Google acts only as a hosting provider, under its own terms. Nothing is sold, shared with third parties, used for advertising or used to build profiles.

The row carries no identifier tied to you. There is no account, no device id, and we do not keep your IP address — in practice we cannot link a report to a person, or join two reports from the same person, except by what is written in them.

If sending fails, the app says so (it never says “sent” when it did not) and offers two ways out, both started by you: opening your e-mail app with a pre-filled message — if you send it, we come to know your address, because it is ordinary e-mail — or copying everything to the clipboard.

How long we keep it, and how to delete it. We keep the row while it is useful for fixing what it describes and, in any case, for at most 24 months. You can ask for it to be deleted sooner by writing to contact@melbees.games. Because the rows carry no identifier, we need your help to find yours: tell us the date you sent it and an excerpt of what you wrote. We delete the matching rows and confirm by e-mail. Without those two details we cannot locate your report — not out of refusal, but because the spreadsheet stores nothing that ties a row to a person.

12. For parents and guardians

Inside the game, under Settings, there is a For parents and guardians page. It asks for no age, sits behind no purchase, and shows:

  • Play time on this device, measured by the game itself. That counter stays on the device and is not sent to us.
  • Where the parental controls are — they are the platform's, not ours: Google Family Link, which can set time limits, and Google Play's purchase approval, which can require authorisation before any purchase.

On Windows, the matching controls are Microsoft's, in Microsoft Family Safety: screen-time limits and approval before a purchase on the Microsoft Store.

The game measures and shows time of use and never interrupts a run. It also sends no notifications, gives no reward for time spent connected, and has no daily streak that is lost by missing a day.

Messages from the game team, only inside the app, with no notification and no conversation. The team writes short messages, signed by the Melbees, kept in Hug's Mail inside the game and read when the player chooses. Nobody answers them and no other player ever writes one; they never appear during a match and are never chosen by age or by how the player plays. Those about the Premium subscription are marked as such and go to everyone, never to one player alone. To fetch the messages for everyone, the game sends our server a request that carries no identifier at all and is the same for every player; what was read stays on the device (section 8 covers a message to one account). A row in Settings > General > Hug brings new messages turns off Hug's arrival with the message; the messages stay in the Mail.

13. Who the data is shared with

We do not sell your data and we do not give it to data brokers. The services below are the only ones that receive anything, each for one purpose:

  • Google Play (Google), on Android — charges for purchases and tells us their state (section 7).
  • Microsoft Store (Microsoft), on Windows: charges for purchases, issues the Microsoft Store ID key and, through it, tells us the subscription's state (section 2.1).

  • Firebase (Google), on Android — analytics and crash reports (section 10).
  • Supabase — authenticates the Google account and hosts the leaderboard database (sections 8 and 9), as our processor.
  • Google Cloud — hosts our server, as our processor.
  • Google Apps Script and Google Sheets — receive and store the feedback reports (section 11), as our processors.

We may also disclose data where the law requires it, to comply with an order from a competent authority, or to investigate fraud and abuse of the leaderboard.

The services above run servers outside Brazil, so the data described here may be processed in other countries, under those providers' contractual safeguards — including the standard contractual clauses that apply to transfers out of the European Union and the United Kingdom.

  • On the device: until you delete it or uninstall the game. The account session, until you sign out (section 8). The server's answer about the account's Premium, until a newer answer replaces it or until you sign out (section 8).

15. Your rights, and how to use them

The LGPD gives you, among others, the rights of confirmation, access, correction, anonymisation, portability, erasure, information about sharing, and withdrawal of consent. Where the GDPR applies, you also have the rights to object, to restrict processing and to portability, and the right to complain to your country's data-protection authority — in Brazil, the ANPD.

In practice:

  • Delete the device data: Settings → Delete my data. It erases progress, settings and the age band you answered, and removes the account session from this device. It does not reach the account.
  • Delete the account: Settings → Account → Delete my account, which immediately deletes the account (with the e-mail, the name, the picture link and the access record the sign-in keeps), the nickname, the portrait, the runs submitted to the leaderboard and the subscription link (on Windows, with the Microsoft Store ID key). Without the game installed, use the Delete your account page, and we act within 30 days. These are two different actions: deleting the account erases nothing on the device, and deleting the device data erases nothing on the account.
  • Delete a feedback report: by e-mail, with the date and an excerpt (section 11).
  • Ads (Android only): change your consent answer in Google's own dialog inside the game, or reset the advertising identifier under Android Settings → Google → Ads.
  • Any other request: contact@melbees.games. We answer within 30 days.

The legal bases. We perform the contract of using the game in order to make the game work, deliver what you bought, and keep your account and the leaderboard (LGPD art. 7 V; GDPR art. 6(1)(b)). We rely on legitimate interests to fix defects, to measure whether the game works, and to defend the leaderboard against cheating (LGPD art. 7 IX; GDPR art. 6(1)(f)). We rely on your consent for personalised advertising where it is required (LGPD art. 7 I; GDPR art. 6(1)(a)), and you may withdraw it at any time. We also comply with legal obligations, such as those of Brazil's Law 15.211/2025, the Digital Statute of the Child and Adolescent (LGPD art. 7 II; GDPR art. 6(1)(c)).

16. Security

Everything that leaves the device travels over HTTPS. Access to the leaderboard database and to the feedback spreadsheet is restricted to us. No system is perfect and we cannot promise absolute security. We hold no password of yours and no payment data, and the game server keeps no IP address, which sharply limits what an incident could expose. What the sign-in keeps about your account is in section 8.

17. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy; the “Last updated” date will change and, for material changes, we will say so inside the app. A new feature that sends data is only switched on after this page has been updated, never before.
